# Story and cast

The story bible for CellGame26 v1. It fixes the premise, the cast, the arc across the 8 levels, how story is shown, the level titles players see, and the art each role needs. Writing every line is left to the cutscene-script tickets. The sample lines here only pin down voices.

Decided with the teacher in [Story and cast](tickets.md#ticket-12). The level list it hangs on is [`docs/levels.md`](levels.md). The accuracy rules it obeys are in [`docs/learning-targets.md`](learning-targets.md).

**Clean room.** The Baseline's cast (two scientists, a robot, a planetary leader) and its beats are used only as evidence of *where* story happens and how often (see `docs/baseline/walkthrough/README.md`). Everything here is ours: none of the Baseline's names, lines, jokes or scenes are reused.

## Premise

- **The player is the cell.** It's a Tardi-Amoeba: an ordinary amoeboid single cell from the Tardigrade home planet. The cast talks *about* it, the way a lab talks about a sample, and never *to* the player as a person.
- **The crisis.** The Tardi-President's "volcano maxxing" policy means lava will cover every habitable part of the planet within days.
- **The plan.** The only craft that can make the 67-light-year trip to Earth is a tiny rocket with room for one petri dish. So the scientists hide a complete Tardi-Settler genome inside the Tardi-Amoeba's DNA as a sleeper payload: a Trojan horse. On arrival it is meant to wake up, so the cell divides and develops into a full-sized humanoid Tardigrade: the first settler on a "totally safe" Earth with hardly any environmental hazards (a climate-change joke).
- **Why the trials.** The host cell is hardy but under-equipped. Before launch it needs organelles and defences, and R.Y.E. insists it is tested properly before it goes in the rocket.
- **The twist is a mistake.** The payload works, but "full size" was set from an enlarged preview image. The settler develops exactly to spec: microscopic, nearly indestructible, next to a water droplet on a blade of grass. Earth doesn't shrink anyone; the scientists got the scale wrong. Earth's lizards and mammals don't notice the Tardi-People for a very long time.

The finale uses *develops* and *specialises*, never *evolves*. Nobody changes over generations: the settler was always going to be the size its genome specified.

## Script rules

These bind every line, in levels and cutscenes.

1. **No evolution language.** The one exception is Ursa's "adapting" line in Level 5, which Aqua corrects on the spot (see `docs/learning-targets.md`, misconceptions). No other line says evolve, evolution, adapt or natural selection.
2. **Viruses are always *viruses*,** never germs or bacteria.
3. **Hydrogen peroxide** (or "peroxide"), never "free radicals". Mitochondria and chloroplasts give it off, and peroxisomes break it down.
4. **The chloroplast uses light *energy* to build glucose from carbon dioxide and water.** Never "makes glucose from light" or "from sunlight".
5. **No Tuning numbers presented as biology.** Story numbers (67 light-years, 5940) are fine because they are obviously story.
6. **Mitochondria and chloroplasts can be absorbed.** Other organelles may simply appear.
7. **No em-dashes in dialogue.** Use full stops, commas or ellipses instead.
8. **Running jokes, used lightly:**
   - the Tardi- prefix (Tardi-Amoeba, Tardi-Settler, Tardi-President, Tardi-Scientist, Tardi-Organelles), which sets up the finale's "Tardi-grade"
   - at most one bread joke per level, from R.Y.E.
   - Gen Alpha slang: "lock in" (Level 5), "mid" (the finale), at least one "67" (the distance to Earth), "cooked", "let him cook" and "aura farming", which the lava, the bread robot and the President set up, and "skill issue" for a loss or Restart in Levels 2+ (never in Level 1, where a struggling student is still learning). Ursa says most of it, R.Y.E. takes it literally, and Aqua lands the shortest line.
   - the lava clock: every cutscene shows the lava closer to the lab
9. **The story never mentions school.** The Class Minimum and the Recap live on the level-select page, not in dialogue. The d.tech campus is only a visual in-joke.

## The arc

| Stretch | Levels | Setting | Who speaks in levels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground trials | 1–4 | The lab, on the Tardigrade planet | Aqua, Ursa, R.Y.E. |
| In flight | 5–6 | The rocket's dish, en route to Earth | The three of them by radio from mission control, while the lava rises |
| On Earth | 7–8 | Snow in the far north, then inside a Canada goose flying south | The same, over a feed that is failing |

**Viruses, by stretch.**
- **Levels 2–4:** R.Y.E. brings them in deliberately, "to make sure it's tough enough for the journey".
- **Levels 5–6:** stowaways from the rushed launch, carried in the dish's supply cargo. R.Y.E. is delighted by the free testing. Viruses don't come from open space: they need a host.
- **Levels 7–8:** Earth viruses, from the snowmelt and then from the goose.

### The Tardi-grade

In the finale R.Y.E. gives the settler "a mid Tardi-grade". It is the same for every player, whatever their Level Ratings: the joke is that R.Y.E.'s standards are absurd. After eight levels his dismissal makes *him* look unreasonable, and Aqua or Ursa gets the comeback, so the last beat still celebrates the mission. The grade category is in the name, and "mid" is the Gen Alpha payoff. It never shows a number and never counts as a Level Rating.

## Presentation

- **In levels:** a dialogue box during a Story Hold. It has the speaker's sprite in an emotion that fits the line, the text, and Back and Next. It works by touch, click and keyboard. In flight and on Earth the sprites sit in a radio or video-feed frame.
- **Cutscenes (between levels):** comic panels. Each is a few sprites composed over a backdrop, with room for dialogue. They advance by tap, click or key, and have Skip and Back. Each is short (about 6 panels at most). They are not built in the game engine.
- **All text is live text,** never baked into images, so screen readers, translation and audio can be added later.
- **Five cutscenes:** Opening, Launch (after Level 4), Arrival (after Level 6), Goose (after Level 7), Finale. Placement roughly follows the original's plan, moved to where our story needs it; each can be replayed from level select once seen.

## Art

**Reference art.** The teacher's own character art, made for this remake: two tardigrade scientists, three poses each, plus cells, organelles and viruses (a Google Drive download from 2023, not yet in the repo). It is clean to use as reference, with no copyright claims. It is not the fork's CC BY-SA art. It covers Aqua (B) and Ursa (A). R.Y.E. has no reference art yet.

**How it's made.** The characters are modelled in Blender from the reference art and rendered in a Spider-Verse-like style into a small set of sprites. Expressions vary on the same model, and the camera can do close-ups, and a high top-down view once the lab roof has melted. The pipeline itself belongs to the art and 3D pipeline work on the map.

**Sprites (about 18).** A sprite needn't show whether the character is mid-sentence.

## Deviations

| Deviation | Pedagogy Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Between-level cutscenes are short comic panels with live text instead of animated movies | Students read at their own pace, and cutscenes count against the classroom-time budget in `docs/levels.md`, so short panels keep more of the period on the learning. |
| The Class Minimum shows on the level-select page rather than as a line in play | The fiction stays uninterrupted, and the teacher reminds the class aloud; the page is where students look when deciding what to do next. |

The new story, cast and titles are not Deviations in themselves: the clean-room rule requires them.
