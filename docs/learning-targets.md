# Learning targets

Resolves [Draft the learning targets the game reinforces](tickets.md#ticket-11). Terms follow `CONTEXT.md`.

## Role in the unit

- **Who plays:** 11th-grade biology. Students have already taken either physics or environmental science, and chemistry.
- **What the game is:** the 5E *Engage* activity. It gives students the experience of "I've seen that happen"; the unit's Explore and Explain phases formalise it afterwards.
- **What a target asks:** that a student can *notice and describe* something in their own words, with the game as their example. None asks for mastery.
- **No over-promising, no over-checking:** too many check-for-understanding moments make the game less engaging, and that defeats the point of an Engage activity.
- **Standards:** the umbrella is NGSS **HS-LS1** (From Molecules to Organisms). We do not map sub-standards or claim to meet any performance expectation.
- **Students who dig deeper:** if they explore the encyclopedia and learn more, that's a bonus. No target depends on it.

## The two Learning Targets

### LT1: Monomers make useful things

*I can name the building blocks my cell collected and say what it made with each.*

The table splits building blocks from fuel, so no row reads as "monomer → polymer" unless it is one.

| Collected | What the cell did with it |
|---|---|
| **Built into larger molecules** | |
| Amino acids | Chained into proteins: enzymes (slicers, DNA repair) and defences (defensins) |
| Nucleotides | Chained into nucleic acids, such as the RNA instructions sent out from the nucleus |
| **Used to make other molecules** | |
| Fatty acids | Used to make the lipids of the membrane and vesicles. Lipids are made *from* fatty acids; they are not chains of them. |
| **Used as fuel** | |
| Glucose | Broken down to make ATP, the cell's energy currency. When there is far more glucose than the cell needs, it is turned into fat. |

### LT2: Organelles do real work

*I can say what an organelle did for my cell, and what went wrong when it was missing, damaged or switched off.*

The contrast is with memorising a list, or building a Jell-O model and relying on analogies. In the game, each organelle's job is something the player needs.

| Organelle | Its job in play |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | Holds the DNA and sends out RNA instructions. If it dies, the cell dies. |
| Ribosome | Builds proteins from amino acids, following RNA instructions |
| Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) | Makes new membrane and starts vesicle products such as peroxisomes |
| Golgi body | Finishes vesicles and sends them where they're needed: lysosomes, defensins, toxin |
| Mitochondrion | Gets far more ATP from each glucose than the cytoplasm can alone (about 38 for the whole pathway, cytoplasm and mitochondrion together). Burns fat as backup fuel. Can divide. Can burn glucose for heat instead of ATP. |
| Chloroplast | Uses light energy to build glucose from carbon dioxide and water. Light supplies the energy; the matter comes from CO₂ and water. |
| Lysosome | Digests and recycles large things, including damaged organelles and invading viruses |
| Peroxisome | Breaks down the hydrogen peroxide that mitochondria and chloroplasts give off, protecting the rest of the cell |
| Centrosome and cytoskeleton | Organise movement (the pseudopod) |
| Plasma membrane | The cell's boundary. It grows with fatty acids, repairs itself using ATP, and holds defensins. At 0 health the cell dies. |

Energy has no target of its own. It is covered by LT1's glucose row and by the mitochondrion and chloroplast rows above.

## Tiers: what may be asked, and how hard

The tiers apply to Field Notes and the Recap alike (see `CONTEXT.md`).

| Tier | Topics | May be asked? |
|---|---|---|
| **Learning Target** | LT1 and LT2 at the level of *what*: what each building block became, what ATP is for, what each organelle did and what broke without it. For example, "What did the peroxisome do?" | Yes, at normal difficulty |
| **Foreshadowed** | Protein synthesis: DNA in the nucleus → RNA → ribosome → protein. Proteins bound for the membrane or for vesicles then continue ER → Golgi → vesicle; the protein makes that trip, not the RNA. Many proteins, including the game's slicers, are finished at ribosomes in the cytoplasm and never enter that route. Students see all this now and learn it properly in the molecular genetics unit next semester. | Only at the easiest level, with at least one obviously wrong option |
| **Experienced** | The *how* and *so what* behind play: what the costs of actions might mean; recycling returning building blocks but not the ATP spent; the chemistry of how hydrogen peroxide damages the cell; virus-defence strategy | Never. Students live through these, and players can think about them, but the game never presents them as facts to learn. |

The boundary is *what* versus *how*: a Learning Target question asks what something did; mechanisms and economics stay Experienced unless they are the Foreshadowed topic.

The instructor's earlier Google Form fits these tiers. Its questions cover:
- **LT1:** ATP as money; glucose as fuel and as a monosaccharide; 2 ATP per glucose without a mitochondrion, about 38 with one; the membrane built from fatty acids; glucose becoming fat when there is too much; slicers being protein.
- **LT2:** the nucleus holds DNA; the ER makes membrane; lysosomes recycle; chloroplasts make glucose (reworded to say from carbon dioxide and water, using light energy).
- **Foreshadowed:** RNA is like a blueprint; RNA goes to a ribosome.
- **Proof of play:** its completion code, the Level 7 temperature and the scientist's hair colour are replaced by the Recap's record of levels completed.

## Science accuracy rules

These rules bind the encyclopedia, tutorials, cutscene scripts, Field Notes and the Recap.

1. **38 ATP stays.** The original game is well balanced, so the number is kept. At the first reveal and in the glossary, a note says: the game uses the older textbook count of 38 ATP per glucose; modern estimates for eukaryotic cells are roughly 30–32. The 38 is described as the yield of the whole respiration pathway (cytoplasm and mitochondrion together), not as ATP made only in the mitochondrion. It is shown as one number, never as +2 and +36.
2. **Glycolysis wording.** In the Baseline economy, the cytoplasm makes ATP only while the cell has no mitochondrion. That economy is kept. No text says or implies that glycolysis stops once mitochondria arrive. Wording such as "a mitochondrion gets far more ATP from each glucose" is correct in both cases.
3. **Lipids are not polymers.** Lipids are made *from* fatty acids. Only proteins and nucleic acids are described as chains of monomers. Turning extra glucose into lipids is described as metabolism, which is what it is.
4. **ATP is a short-term store.** ATP is the energy currency and fat is the longer-term store. The game's ATP ceiling is a game number and is never presented as biology.
5. **No Tuning numbers in the encyclopedia.** Entries define the biology (what a pseudopod is, for example) and never the game rule (how the pseudopod's cost is worked out). The same applies to other Tuning numbers and formulas, such as resource pickups that appear only while the cell moves, or 100% refunds on recycling.
6. **Where organelles come from.**
   - The mitochondrion and chloroplast are absorbed from outside, as in the Baseline. The small print gives their real origin in endosymbiosis.
   - Other organelles may simply appear the first time. The small print reminds students of cell theory ("all cells come from other cells") and says how organelles really arise.
7. **Slicers.** Slicers *work like* RNA interference in eukaryotic cells, and *do a similar job to* CRISPR in bacteria: they find the invader's genetic code and cut it. The encyclopedia never calls them relatives or "cousins" of either, and never says slicers *are* either one. The cell is a semi-fictional alien, and "works like" is as far as the claim goes.
8. **No carbohydrate polymer.** Glucose appears only as a monomer. Storing surplus glucose as fat is the game's storage story. Real amoebae also store glycogen, which the instructor can use as a bridge in the Explain phase. No glycogen or starch mechanic is added.
9. **Hydrogen peroxide, not "free radicals".** The Baseline's green particles are called **hydrogen peroxide** (or "peroxide") everywhere a player sees them: HUD, objectives, tutorials and the encyclopedia. Mitochondria and chloroplasts really do give it off, and peroxisomes really do break it down, so the mechanic stays as it is. The name also explains the organelle: *perox*isomes deal with *perox*ide. The encyclopedia adds that it is one of several *reactive oxygen species*. Its small print notes the simplification: the game shows new peroxisomes budding from the ER, which is one real route; real cells also make them by dividing existing peroxisomes. (Corrected in [Specify Level 3 — Membrane](tickets.md#ticket-64): the game has no Golgi step for peroxisomes.)
10. **Light is energy, not matter.** Chloroplasts use light *energy* to build glucose from carbon dioxide and water. No text says a chloroplast makes glucose "from light" or "from sunlight" alone.

## Misconceptions the game must not reinforce

| Misconception | Where the risk is | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Individuals "adapt" (evolve) within a lifetime | The Baseline has a character say the cell "is adapting" | No evolution language or mechanics. If the line is reused, it is countered immediately with something witty and memorable, not a lecture. |
| Viruses are bacteria | Every enemy is a virus, and the next unit is Humans vs. Bacteria | Always say *virus*; never "germs" or "bacteria" for these enemies |
| Glycolysis stops once a cell has mitochondria | The Baseline economy | Accuracy rule 2 |
| ATP is how cells store energy long-term | ATP can pile up to a high ceiling | Accuracy rule 4 |
| Game rules are biology | Pseudopod cost formula, pickups that appear only while moving, 100% refunds | Accuracy rule 5 |
| Organelles come from nowhere | Organelles appear or are absorbed | Accuracy rule 6 |
| Light turns into sugar (matter from energy) | Chloroplasts appear to make glucose from nothing but light | Accuracy rule 10 |
| Harmful chemicals only come from outside the cell | — | The Baseline already gets this right: hydrogen peroxide is a by-product of the cell's own ATP and glucose making. Keep it. |

## Tie-ins, noted and not chased

- **Marathon Runner (homeostasis).** Cellular respiration:
  - why we need oxygen, and how muscles use blood glucose while running
  - ATP demand rising with activity
  - fat as backup fuel when glucose runs out (the mitochondrion's fat-burning backup)
  - the membrane spending ATP to repair itself
- **Humans vs. Bacteria.**
  - The membrane as a barrier.
  - Defensins (real antimicrobial peptides).
  - Lysosomes digesting invaders (a bridge to phagocytosis).
  - Viruses hijacking ribosomes, as the contrast to bacteria.
  - Slicers, whose defence is closer to a bacterium's (CRISPR) than to a human immune system's.

  - **Pseudopod Deviation.** The preview arrow and cost are clamped to the lens, and the charge matches the movement actually bought. Pedagogy Rationale: *students read ATP as the price of a visible action; charging for distance the cell never travels makes ATP spending look arbitrary.*
  - **Balance impact.** At 15 nodes, the most a pseudopod can cost becomes about 57 ATP. Over a run like the walkthrough's Level 1, the saving is roughly 18 ATP.
  - **Balance check.** A Balance Run checks that Level 1's time-to-win stays within its range.
  - **Renaming Deviation.** The Baseline's "free radicals" become **hydrogen peroxide** in every player-visible text. Pedagogy Rationale: *students should learn the correct term, and "peroxide" explains what peroxisomes are for.* Mechanics and numbers are unchanged.
